What we are doing now

Our current work is on materials with grain boundaries, the interfaces where two crystals of the same material meet. We want to know how these boundaries change the way heat moves through a material.

  1. Build the structures

    We generate new atomic models of grain boundaries to simulate.

  2. Fit the potentials

    We develop the interatomic potentials that make these large structures possible to simulate.

  3. Run the pipeline

    One workflow links established simulation programs, so each new study is easier to set up.

  4. Compute thermal conductivity

    The property we are specialised in right now.

About Us

Mission
New Materials Lab is an initiative focused on the understanding, design and optimization of new materials using computer simulations.
Approach
The discovery and implementation of new materials and technologies in industry requires the fast and efficient search and prediction of material properties and functionalities. At New Materials Lab, we strive to combine first-principles electronic structure theory and multi-scale methods with the development of robust high-throughput frameworks and data-driven approaches to accelerate materials discovery.
Application
We predict and explore the optoelectronic and temperature-dependent properties of materials related to renewable energies and other technologies with a relevant impact in our society, such as thermoelectricity, solar cells, catalysis or aerospace.

Research Projects

New Materials Lab is an initiative created by European Commission funding through Marie-Curie Action: "High throughput computing for accelerated photovoltaic material discovery: from material database to the new generation of photovoltaic materials" (HT-PHOTO-DB: H2020-752608). This project is based on the combination of databases with development of high-throughput frameworks to accelerate the discovery of new and more efficient materials for photovoltaic devices. However, New Materials Lab is also involved in other projects in which computational materials science and data-driven approaches can be applied to accelerate the discovery of new materials.
