How far can a computed XPS peak be trusted?

We compare the separation between the O 1s and C 1s peaks of the same molecule, measured in the same experiment. This removes the experimental charge-referencing error, so what remains is the method's own uncertainty. It falls from 1.7 eV to 0.23 eV with the best approach.

Theory untangles a crowded spectrum

Solid citric acid has three kinds of carbon and three kinds of oxygen, so its C 1s and O 1s peaks overlap. We set the spacing between the fitted components with DFT energy differences and their uncertainty, so each component has a clear chemical meaning.

A handful of calculations is enough to train the model

We predict the lattice thermal conductivity of CoSb3 with a machine learning model trained on a small number of supercell calculations. The error in the forces drops quickly, and the conductivity settles from about eight supercells.

Where p-type CoSb3 works best

The thermoelectric figure of merit, zT, depends on temperature and on how many charge carriers the material has. The calculated zT of p-type CoSb3 peaks near 1.0 at high temperature and a carrier concentration around 2 × 1019 cm−3.
